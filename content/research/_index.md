# Rent-Sharing and Worker Mobility at Innovative Firms (with Sabien Dobbelaere)

We provide individual-level causal evidence that firms share innovation-induced profit gains with incumbent workers, and use this rent-sharing as a retention device. Exploiting the staggered introduction of the Dutch Innovation Box — which cuts the effective corporate tax rate on qualifying innovation profits from 25% to 5% — we study how this large, firm-level surplus shock affects the workers already employed at innovating firms before they received the tax break. Combining matched employer-employee administrative data covering the full Dutch workforce (2007–2023) with a tailored k-nearest-neighbor matching procedure and a staggered difference-in-differences estimator, we compare incumbent workers at treated firms to matched incumebts control firms. We find that incumbent workers at treated firms earn 4.3% higher annual base wages after treatment relative to matched controls, and that this wage premium persists and grows over a ten-year post-treatment window. The effect is not confined to R&D personnel. We further show that treated incumbents are 1.6% less likely to separate from their employer in the long run, consistent with firms sharing rents strategically to retain workers who embody firm-specific knowledge accumulated during the innovation process. Our findings link corporate tax incidence, innovation policy evaluation, and rent-sharing literature.

# Measuring Advertising Spillovers in the Automobile Industry (with Jose-Luis Moraga Gonzalez and Sandor Zsolt)

This paper examines the existence of advertising spillovers in the automobile industry. For this purpose, we formulate an empirical consideration set model of demand where advertising serves to increase buyer awareness. Each car model has an independent probability of being considered for purchase and this probability is influenced by a brand's own advertising, as well as the advertising of substitute models within the same brand, other brands within the same business group, and competing brands. We estimate the model using data from the Netherlands on regional car sales, prices, car advertising expenditures and car characteristics for the year 2008. We find evidence that own advertising increases the consideration probability of a car and that advertising spillovers are positive and sizable. These spillovers have important implications for firm strategy and market outcomes.
